# Meteorites and Bolides: A Data‑Driven Exploration of NASA Open Datasets

In the vast field of aerospace engineering and space sciences, the exploration of celestial phenomena remains a source of fascination and inquiry. Using the power of data science, this project analyzes two compelling datasets from NASA’s Open Data Portal: Meteorite Landings and Fireball and Bolide Reports. The goal is to uncover patterns, extract meaningful insights, and contribute to a deeper understanding of extraterrestrial events through a data‑driven lens.

## Meteorite Dataset (DF2)

The Meteorite Landings dataset contains a rich collection of attributes that describe meteorites recovered across the globe. Each field offers a different perspective on the physical characteristics, origins, and impact patterns of these extraterrestrial objects.

The dataset includes the meteorite’s name, ID, nametype, recclass, mass in grams, fall status, year of landing, latitude, longitude, and geolocation. Together, these attributes provide a comprehensive view of the nature, magnitude, and timing of meteorite events. The name and ID serve as unique identifiers that allow for consistent referencing. Nametype and recclass describe classification and composition, offering insight into formation and material properties. Mass provides a quantitative measure of size and potential impact significance. The fall attribute indicates whether the meteorite was observed falling or simply found, which helps distinguish dynamic atmospheric entries from later discoveries. The year field captures the temporal sequence of landings, enabling trend analysis across decades or centuries. Latitude, longitude, and geolocation anchor each event spatially, supporting geospatial mapping and regional pattern identification.

## Bolide Dataset (DF1)

The Fireball and Bolide Reports dataset provides a chronological record of bright atmospheric events detected by U.S. Government sensors. While not exhaustive, it captures many of the most energetic bolides observed globally.

Each entry includes the date and time of peak brightness in Coordinated Universal Time, along with the latitude and longitude of the event. Altitude describes the height at which the bolide reached maximum luminosity, offering insight into atmospheric entry behavior. Velocity, expressed in kilometers per second, quantifies the speed of the object as it traveled through the atmosphere. The velocity components vx, vy, and vz provide a vector breakdown of its motion, revealing directional dynamics. Total radiated energy measures the luminous output of the event, while calculated total impact energy estimates the overall energy release, offering context for the scale and potential consequences of these high‑energy atmospheric phenomena.

## Project Purpose

By combining machine learning, exploratory data analysis, and geospatial visualization, this project seeks to illuminate the behavior of meteorites and bolides as they interact with Earth. Through careful examination of NASA’s open datasets, the work aims to bridge aerospace engineering curiosity with data‑driven scientific insight, contributing to a clearer understanding of the cosmic material that reaches our planet.
